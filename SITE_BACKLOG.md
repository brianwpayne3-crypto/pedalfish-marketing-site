# PedalFish Marketing Site Backlog

Ideas to revisit as the V1 marketing site evolves. These are not commitments to build everything now, and we should not make PedalFish look like a mature SaaS company before the product supports the claims.

## Site sections / pages to consider

### Product
The homepage already tells the core product story well. Over time, deeper product pages may make sense for areas such as Service and broader Shop Operations as those capabilities become real.

### About
Important for PedalFish because the origin story is part of the differentiation: the product is being built from actual work inside independent bike shops, by people doing the work. Avoid generic corporate/team-page language.

### Integrations
Create when there is enough substance to be useful. It can distinguish clearly among **available**, **being worked on**, and **planned** rather than implying unshipped integrations already exist.

Known integration directions include Square and future parts/distributor/sourcing connections. Only describe specific capabilities when their status is accurate.

### Pricing / Early Access
Do not invent conventional SaaS tiers before pricing is understood. Before final pricing exists, a legitimate **Early Access** page can explain that PedalFish is working with a small number of shops and that pricing is still being developed.

### For Shops / Who it's for
Potentially explain how PedalFish fits independent shops, owner-operators, mobile mechanics, and different shop operating models without assuming every bike shop works the same way.

### Contact / Early Access
Eventually provide a proper destination/form rather than relying indefinitely on a single email link.

### Sign in
Once `app.pedal-fish.com` is established, add a persistent Sign in path from the marketing site to the actual product. This is both useful and evidence that a real application exists behind the marketing site.

### Privacy + Terms
Add before/while inviting real businesses and customers into the product. Footer links are sufficient; these do not need marketing prominence.

### Product direction / where PedalFish is going
Consider a section or page that explains the broader progression without calling it a public roadmap or claiming unshipped functionality:

`understand repairs → customers / bikes / parts / work in progress → help the owner understand and operate the whole shop`

This can communicate that service management is the operational foundation rather than the eventual product boundary.

## Things not to add merely to look established

Avoid fake maturity signals such as:

- empty Resources sections;
- a blog with token posts just to have a blog;
- customer logos without permission;
- testimonials that have not been earned;
- giant feature matrices;
- Enterprise pages without an actual enterprise offering;
- invented integrations or capabilities;
- artificial SaaS pricing tiers.

The goal is to look like a legitimate real product/company while being accurate about the stage PedalFish is actually in.

## Possible future navigation

A plausible next navigation once the supporting content exists:

`Product · Integrations · About · Early Access | Sign in`

Pricing can enter when there is enough learning to say something useful.

## Founder / LinkedIn milestone

Brian is considering changing his LinkedIn experience to include **Founder / Technical Founder — PedalFish**, potentially alongside rather than instead of his Sulcata consulting/advisory work.

Current hesitation is not whether Brian is actually building the product; it is whether PedalFish feels sufficiently real and professional publicly to make the founder identity feel earned and credible.

Do not force an arbitrary launch date. Revisit this as a product/company milestone. Useful signals to consider include:

- `pedal-fish.com` presents a coherent professional product story;
- the actual application is reachable through a deliberate product URL such as `app.pedal-fish.com`;
- brand and product presentation feel intentional rather than experimental;
- there is a clear way for a real shop to contact Brian / request early access;
- at least one real shop workflow is being run through PedalFish and the product is grounded in actual operating use;
- Brian can describe in a sentence what PedalFish is, who it is for, and what exists today without leaning on speculative future capability.

This does **not** require final pricing, a mature integrations catalog, a large customer base, or a fully finished SaaS website. The question is whether the public product has crossed from private experiment into something Brian is intentionally putting into the world.

When revisiting LinkedIn, consider whether **Founder** or **Founder & Builder / Technical Founder** best communicates the work without making PedalFish sound larger or more mature than it is. It is also reasonable for PedalFish and Sulcata to coexist as concurrent experience if both are genuinely active.
